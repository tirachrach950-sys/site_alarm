Sleep Cycles Explained

A night of sleep is not one long block. It is a series of cycles, each lasting about 90 minutes.

The stages of a sleep cycle

  • Light sleep (stages 1 and 2): your heart rate slows and your body relaxes. You wake easily.
  • Deep sleep (stage 3): the body repairs itself. Waking from deep sleep leaves you groggy.
  • REM sleep: the stage with vivid dreams. It is important for memory and mood.

How many cycles do you need?

Most adults need 5 cycles (7.5 hours). Some do well with 6 (9 hours), and 4 (6 hours) is a short night. Cycle length varies from person to person and from night to night, usually between 70 and 120 minutes.

Why plan around cycles?

When your alarm rings during deep sleep you feel groggy, which is called sleep inertia. When it rings at the end of a cycle, in light sleep, waking up is easier. The sleep calculator works out those times for you.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to fall asleep?

Most healthy adults need about 10 to 20 minutes. This calculator uses 15 minutes. If you usually fall asleep faster or slower, shift the times by the difference.

What is a sleep cycle?

A sleep cycle lasts about 90 minutes and moves through light sleep, deep sleep and REM sleep. Waking at the end of a cycle usually feels easier than waking in the middle of deep sleep.

How many hours of sleep do adults need?

Most adults do best with 7 to 9 hours. That is 5 or 6 full cycles. Needs vary from person to person.
